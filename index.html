Abstract

Multi-arm robot platforms, including bimanual and humanoid robots, have the capability to complete tasks with lower cycle times than single-arm systems, but they are more difficult to safely control due to the presence of multi-arm constraints. Imitation learning from demonstrations is effective for controlling bimanual systems; however, it requires large datasets of demonstrations, which are expensive to collect via teleoperation.

Automated demonstration generation can alleviate this burden by synthesizing new demonstrations from a handful of source demonstrations, but existing algorithms ignore multi-arm constraints and as a result often induce unsafe and unsuccessful behaviors. We address this limitation by introducing ScheduleGen, an integrated planning & scheduling algorithm for multi-arm demonstration generation. ScheduleGen first plans single-robot behavior that adapts object-centric skill demonstrations and then safely coordinates the robots by framing scheduling as a Mixed Integer Linear Programming (MILP) with constraints evaluated using GPU-accelerated collision checking.

We evaluate ScheduleGen on 28 simulation task variants, including seven externally authored RoboDojo tasks and settings inaccessible to prior systems; it achieves 79.6% mean data-generation success and trains policies with 65.5% mean success. These policies outperform the DexMimicGen baseline on almost all the tasks and achieve 65–80% success across five real-world tasks through zero-shot sim-to-real and real-to-sim-to-real transfer.
